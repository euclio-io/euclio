## 1 · What this is (CLAUDE.md "What this is" paragraph, verbatim)

> Euclio watches the automations a freelancer/small agency runs for their clients (n8n, Make, Zapier, custom scripts) from both ends: a heartbeat ping says it ran, and a canary address riding silently in the send list confirms that what the client's customers receive actually arrived. The moment either signal breaks, the freelancer knows first — before their client does. Every catch, resolution, verified arrival, and quiet day lands in a per-client ledger, so the freelancer can answer any "is it working?" question in one message, with receipts, and walk into every renewal with the record in hand. When a catch is worth telling, they compose a note from the facts in their own words — optional, never automated. Euclio sells certainty to the freelancer: never be caught not knowing. The client is non-technical and never uses Euclio directly.

## 2 · Vision, mission, intention

The buyer's feeling is the product. Euclio sells the freelancer's certainty — the end of the background dread of running many clients' automations — not client impressions. Value never routes through unvalidated client psychology; every core claim is testable directly with the buyer. The three dread moments define the product: something breaks and I don't know (heartbeat + canary), the client asks and I scramble (the ledger's answer), renewal comes and I can't prove anything (the record as receipt). The client's experience of Euclio is silence, until a moment where evidence decides money — a worried question or a renewal — and then a devastating, exact answer from the freelancer. Presence through evidence, not broadcasts. Positioning line family: "You'll know first" / "Bad news should never come from your client" / "Never be caught not knowing" (internal). Category refusal: Euclio is not filed with monitoring tools (Healthchecks, Cronitor, Sentry) nor with deliverability dashboards; its unit is the ledger, not the incident.

## 3 · Principles (append-by-name; the plan carries its own numbering)

**HONESTY.** Generated text states only observations. Interpretation, severity, and reassurance are human-only; "nothing was missed" may only be said by a human who checked. facts.ts bans severity words, inference ("missed", "would have", "were affected"), recovery-time promises, abstract-pain phrases, passive constructions; /fail adds the fact shape "reported a failure at 9:02am" as distinct from "stopped checking in at 9:02am."
**THE FREELANCER SENDS.** Euclio never contacts a client. No client-facing surfaces, logins, dashboards, or the word "Euclio" client-side; `/u/[publicSlug]` exists only as an optional attachable receipt.
**THE LEDGER IS THE PRODUCT.** Incident and rollup data is never pruned below 12 months; per-workflow daily rollups preserve the math when raw pings and receipts are pruned.
**THE DATA PRINCIPLE.** Diagnostics are opt-in per workflow, default off; consent and mechanism are one artifact (the checkbox changes the generated snippet). Layered scrub: truncate ~200 chars → client-side pattern redaction visible in the snippet → server-side second pass at ingest (the only pass for platform users) → hard cap → 30-day TTL nightly purge. errorText renders only in the freelancer's incident view and structurally cannot reach a ClientUpdate. Copy ceiling: "layered and deleted," never "we never receive it" or "we catch everything."
**THE CANARY OBSERVES, IT DOES NOT INFER.** A receipt means the canary received a copy. Euclio never claims any other recipient received anything; "arrived" in generated text always means "arrived at the canary." Per-recipient delivery is the human's judgment. The canary inherits the data principle wholesale: headers and subject hash only, bodies transient for FF1 checks, receipts pruned with raw pings, content firewalled from ClientUpdate.
**NEVER IN THE CRITICAL PATH.** Euclio observes; generated snippets fail open; an Euclio outage must be invisible to client automations. The v2 kill-switch is the sole permitted touch of the critical path and defaults to *run* on any timeout or error — its honest promise is "stops the bleeding while Euclio is reachable," stated wherever it is described.

## 4 · The product, by tier

**Launch scope (frozen — nothing enters unless a partner asks or a public claim requires it).** Heartbeat: one check-in per workflow at `POST /ping/{token}` (GET also accepted), grace period, silence detection with debounce. Explicit failure: `POST /ping/{token}/fail` opens an incident immediately with no debounce, storm-guarded per incident; the snippet's catch-block pings it, and n8n/Make error handlers get a copy-paste variant mapping the error-message field only. Diagnostics: opt-in scrubbed error text per the data principle. The canary (slice M5.2): per-workflow inbound address, an expectation rule ("weekdays by 9:05" plus lateness window), receipt logging, gap accounting (sends due in an incident window vs receipts matched, denormalized onto the incident), and the first-matched-receipt milestone. The ledger + answer view (M5.5): per-client register of incidents, quiet runs, and milestones; expanded incident entries with events and receipts; a factual summary assembled from the record with a required, empty "your read" slot; Copy; optional note composition per the note spec (slot 2 human-only and mandatory before send); Share receipt. Snippet dashboard (M1): curl/Node/Python tabs plus a fourth "copy for your coding agent" tab, token pre-filled, live "✓ test ping received" confirmation.

**Fast-follows.** FF1 content integrity (merge-field render and subject-pattern checks on the canary copy). FF2 onboarding kit at client creation (expectation-setter, "what runs for you" map, canary setup, `euclio-init` as an optional Node generator of the canonical snippet, the agent setup doc). FF3 note channel variants.

**v2.** Kill-switch (fail-open per principle above; prefer the platform-API pause variant on n8n/Make, which never touches the run path), pre-failure radar (token expiries, deprecations), timing drift, cadence baseline, renewal recap, and the post-validation AI layers: operator-side triage and morning digest, payload heuristics for non-send workflows (opt-in counts/durations only), per-operator voice matching, and the AI-tell linter (advisory, never blocking).

## 5 · Packaging & delivery doctrine (summary; full record in the addendum §11)

Snippet, not SDK: the custom-path mechanism is a dashboard-generated, readable, copy-paste snippet — try/catch wrapped, 3–5s timeout, one retry at most, awaited, zero dependencies — never a published package (its worst failure, our code crashing the automation we're paid to watch, inverts the promise). Platforms use the native HTTP module; no listed integrations. All copy says "one check-in," never "one line." Footprint claim ceiling: "the whole footprint is one request" — never "zero overhead"; on Zapier the ping consumes a task, acknowledged in docs. The snippet shown on the landing page is the canonical contract and must never diverge from what the dashboard generates. SDK reopening condition: only if the sensor deepens toward structured payloads or agent traces, from an installed base of trust.

## 6 · Build order

Then **M5.2 The canary** (address, expectation, receipts, gap accounting; verify: a simulated pause spanning two due sends ends with a ledger line reading "2 due in the gap, 2 arrived"). Definition-of-done loop: add workflow with client-facing name → test ping ✓ → watcher → alert → fail ping opens instantly → canary receipt matched → gap computed → ledger entry → answer a simulated client question from the answer view → optionally compose and send a note → all-green. ## 7 · App design direction

The product's native layout is a **register, not an issue page**: a per-client chronological ledger where quiet runs and milestones are substantive rows and an incident is an entry expanded inline (events and receipts side by side, summary + required read slot + actions in the entry footer). Structural kinship: Stripe's activity log and a bank statement — financial-trust software — never Sentry's issue-detail genre. Differentiate at the structural level, stay conventional at the control level (tables, chips, filters, kebabs). Icon rail for clients; workflows are tags and filters within a client's ledger, not the page's unit. Design spec and single source for landing screenshots: **`euclio-answer-view.html`**. Known limit to note in the M5.5 prompt: inline expansion holds to roughly ten events; beyond that, open a focused panel.

## 9 · Validation queue & blind spots

Before claims travel further: time one real partner setup against the "two minutes" line; prototype `/fail` through an n8n error workflow before the URL scheme calcifies. Discovery-call questions: worried-question frequency and clustering around new clients; "when you take on a new client, how do you know the first sends actually landed?"; "would you add an npm/pip package from a new tool into a client's codebase, or would you rather paste a few lines?"; "when a workflow fails, what do you do in the first five minutes, and would an AI-drafted diagnosis with linked evidence save real time?"; "would you ever let a tool send your client an update without you touching it?" Wedge: "just landed a new client." Blind spots carried (documentation problems, not SDK-solvable): a snippet pasted before the work completes lies healthy; serverless edge cases can drop an awaited ping; partial completion past the ping node is invisible; an edited-out try/catch restores crash risk; the canary covers only send-type workflows (the heartbeat covers the rest — never anchor the whole promise on delivery proof).

## 10 · AI strategy — below the waterline

AI reads the machines; the human talks to the client. In a market where every adjacent tool races AI into the client-facing artifact while the client-side backlash grows, the absence is the feature: **no client-facing AI, ever** — no AI-authored client meaning, no auto-send, no machine severity, no client chatbot — as policy and as marketing. Layers, sequenced by validated pull: **L0 (now)** the MVP ships with zero LLM; deterministic facts.ts is the brand seed. **L1 (post-validation)** operator-side honest triage: explain-the-failure with every claim linked to a raw event and a stated "what I did not check"; the ran-right sensor split by coverage (canary receipts primary for send-type workflows at launch; opt-in payload counts/durations for the rest, deterministic heuristics before any ML); a morning digest ranking attention without severity labels. **L2 (with M5.5's flow, LLM later)** the voice guard: the required human line is already structural; voice matching learns only from that operator's own sent updates; the AI-tell linter advises and never blocks. **L3 (call option)** agent oversight, zero spend until a partner asks. The under-appreciated asset: the skeleton-vs-sent diff corpus — per-tenant, consent-by-use, accruing from M5.5 day one.

**AI safety contract** (in code, not policy, for any AI feature ever): facts-linked · negative space declared · no machine severity · human-final · tenant-scoped (no cross-account learning without explicit anonymized opt-in) · provenance-labeled (operator UI always distinguishes AI-suggested from human-authored). Monetization: flat through validation, tier later; safety mechanics are never the paid part. Full analysis and scoring: `Euclio_AI_strategy.md`.
